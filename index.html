01

Introduction

Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (14 April 1891 – 6 December 1956), popularly known as Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar, was an Indian polymath, jurist, economist, politician, writer and social reformer. He inspired the Dalit Buddhist movement and campaigned against social discrimination towards the untouchables (Dalits). He also supported the rights of workers, farmers and women.

He was independent India’s first Law and Justice Minister, the father of the Indian Constitution and one of the architects of the Republic of India. He earned doctorates in economics from both Columbia University and the London School of Economics.

Early in his career he was a professor of economics and also practised law; the later part of his life was devoted largely to politics. Fed up with the evils of Hindu society and untouchability, he embraced Buddhism in 1956. In 1990 he was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna.

His birthday on 14 April is celebrated as Ambedkar Jayanti, and 6 December is observed as Mahaparinirvan Diwas.

02

Early Life

Ambedkar was born on 14 April 1891 in the Mhow military cantonment in the Central Provinces of British India (now Madhya Pradesh). He was the 14th and last child of Ramji Maloji Sakpal and Bhimabai. The family, of Marathi origin, followed the Kabir Panth and came from Ambadawe village in Ratnagiri district.

He belonged to the Hindu Mahar caste, then considered untouchable, and so faced deep social and economic discrimination. His father, Ramji Sakpal, served in the Indian Army at the Mhow cantonment and rose to the rank of subedar.

On 7 November 1900 his name was entered at the Government High School, Satara, as ‘Bhiva Ramji Ambadawekar’. Later a teacher, Krishna Keshav Ambedkar, out of affection gave him his own surname ‘Ambedkar’. He has been known by that name ever since. In memory of that day, 7 November is celebrated in Maharashtra as “Students’ Day”.

In 1896, when he was five, his mother died and his aunt Mirabai looked after him. In April 1906, at about 15, he was married to nine-year-old Ramabai. When he passed the fourth English standard, his success was publicly celebrated among the untouchables, and Dada Keluskar presented him with a ‘Life of the Buddha’, through which he first learnt about the Buddha and Buddhism.

03

Education

Mumbai

In 1897 the family moved to Mumbai, where he studied at the Government High School on Elphinstone Road. He passed matriculation in 1907 and the next year entered Elphinstone College (affiliated to Bombay University) — the first from his community to reach that level. In 1912 he earned his B.A. in economics and political science.

Columbia University, New York

In 1913, aged 22, he went to the USA on a scholarship ($11.50 a month) from Sayajirao Gaekwad of Baroda. In June 1915 he earned his M.A. — economics as the major, with sociology, history, philosophy and anthropology. He was influenced by John Dewey and his work on democracy. In 1916 he completed a second thesis, ‘National Dividend of India’, and the Ph.D. thesis ‘Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India’ (degree formally conferred in 1927). He finished a three-year scholarship’s course in just two years.

London

In October 1916 he went to London — the Bar course at Gray’s Inn and a doctorate at the London School of Economics. In June 1917 he had to return when his scholarship ended; on the way back, the ship carrying his books was sunk by a German submarine torpedo. In 1920, with help from Shahu Maharaj and friends, he went to England again and earned his M.Sc. in 1921, was called as Barrister-at-Law in 1922 and received his D.Sc. in 1923 (thesis: “The Problem of the Rupee”). On the way home he also studied for three months at the University of Bonn in Germany.

05

The Fight Against Untouchability

“Untouchability is worse than slavery.”— Dr. Ambedkar

Even as Military Secretary to the Maharaja of Baroda he had to quit soon because of caste discrimination (described in his autobiography ‘Waiting for a Visa’). His attempts as an accountant, private tutor and investment consultant failed once clients learned he was an untouchable. In 1918 he became professor of political economy at Sydenham College, but other professors objected to his sharing the drinking-water pot.

In 1920 he launched the weekly ‘Mooknayak’. Shahu Maharaj of Kolhapur was deeply impressed. Practising at the Bombay High Court, he founded the ‘Bahishkrit Hitkarini Sabha’, aimed at education and socio-economic uplift.

On 1 January 1927 he held a ceremony at the Koregaon Victory Memorial (Jaystambh). In 1927 he led the Mahad Satyagraha to win the untouchables’ right to draw water from the Chavdar Tank, and on 25 December 1927 he burned copies of the Manusmriti with thousands of followers (the day is remembered as ‘Manusmriti Dahan Diwas’).

In 1930, after three months of preparation, he launched the Kalaram Temple Satyagraha (Nashik), which drew about 15,000 volunteers. When the procession reached the temple gate, the doors were shut.

06

The Round Table Conferences and the Poona Pact

At the First Round Table Conference in London on 8 August 1930, Ambedkar laid out his political vision — that the safety of the oppressed lies in their being independent of both the government and the Congress. At the Second Round Table Conference in 1931 he had a sharp debate with Gandhi over separate electorates for the untouchables.

In 1932 the British government announced the Communal Award, giving the Depressed Classes separate electorates and two votes. Gandhi began a fast unto death in Yerwada jail. Amid mounting pressure Ambedkar reached Yerwada on 24 September 1932 and the Poona Pact was signed.

07

Political Life

Ambedkar’s political career began in 1926, when the Governor of Bombay nominated him to the Legislative Council (till 1936). On 13 October 1935 he became Principal of the Government Law College. In Mumbai he built the three-storey house ‘Rajgruha’, whose private library held over 50,000 books — then the largest private library in the world. His wife Ramabai died on 27 May 1935.

1936

Independent Labour Party founded; it won 13 seats in the 1937 elections. He served as Leader of the Opposition in the Bombay Assembly.

Lost the Lok Sabha election; nominated to the Rajya Sabha in March 1952.

Announced the founding of the Republican Party of India; it was formed after him, in 1957.

After 1940 he wrote books such as ‘Thoughts on Pakistan’ and ‘What Congress and Gandhi Have Done to the Untouchables?’, and in ‘Who Were the Shudras?’ explained the origin of the Shudras.

08

Making the Constitution

After independence on 15 August 1947, Ambedkar was invited to become the country’s first Law and Justice Minister. On 29 August 1947 he was appointed Chairman of the Drafting Committee. He studied the constitutions of about 60 countries and is regarded as the “Father of the Indian Constitution”.

“Mr. President… the burden of drafting this Constitution fell on Dr. Ambedkar, and I have no doubt that we are grateful to him.”— T.T. Krishnamachari, in the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949. It guarantees freedom of religion, abolishes untouchability and bars all forms of discrimination; the reservation of jobs and education for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and other backward classes is linked to his advocacy. Granville Austin called it “first and foremost a social document”.

“If anything goes wrong, it will not be because our Constitution is bad, but because the man who uses it is vile.”— Dr. Ambedkar

Other matters: he opposed Article 370 and recommended a Uniform Civil Code. When the Hindu Code Bill (giving women rights in inheritance, marriage and more) was held up in Parliament in 1951, he resigned from the Cabinet.

09

Conversion and the Buddhist Dhamma

On 13 October 1935, at Yeola near Nashik, Ambedkar declared:

“Though I was born an untouchable Hindu, I will not die a Hindu!”

He then spent 21 years studying all the major religions. He chose Buddhism because it unites prajna (reason), karuna (compassion) and samata (equality). He turned down inducements worth crores of rupees from the Nizam of Hyderabad and several missionaries.

In 1955 he founded the ‘Bharatiya Bauddha Mahasabha’ and in 1956 completed ‘The Buddha and His Dhamma’ (published 1957). On 14 October 1956 at Nagpur he embraced Buddhism, taking the Triple Refuge and Five Precepts from Mahasthavir Chandramani, and gave about five lakh followers diksha into Navayana Buddhism with the 22 vows. On 15 October another 2–3 lakh and on 16 October at Chandrapur about 3 lakh followed — over 11 lakh people in three days. The ground at Nagpur became known as Deekshabhoomi.

“I consider the Dhamma of the Buddha the best. No religion can be compared with it.”

Contribution to Economics

Ambedkar was among the first Indians to earn a doctorate in economics abroad. He stressed industrialisation and agricultural development, and treated education, sanitation, community health and housing as basic amenities.

Reserve Bank of India

The RBI was founded on his ideas, which he submitted to the Hilton Young Commission.

Three theses

Administration and Finance of the East India Company · Provincial Finance in British India · The Problem of the Rupee.

Personal Life

His grandfather was Maloji Sakpal, father Ramji Sakpal and mother Bhimabai. Ramabai and Bhimrao had five children — four sons (Yashwant, Ramesh, Gangadhar, Rajratna) and a daughter (Indu); all but Yashwant died in childhood. Ramabai died in 1935.

On 15 April 1948 he married Dr. Sharada Kabir in New Delhi; she took the name Savita Ambedkar. Known as ‘Mai’ or ‘Maisaheb’, she died on 29 May 2003. His grandson Prakash Ambedkar is active in politics.

13

Books and Writings

Ambedkar was a gifted and combative writer. His English works include 32 books and monographs (22 complete, 10 incomplete), 10 memoranda, evidences and statements, 10 research papers and 10 forewords. ‘The Buddha and His Dhamma’ is regarded as the scripture of Indian Buddhists, and his D.Sc. thesis is linked to the founding of the Reserve Bank of India. The Maharashtra government has published 22 volumes (till 2019) of ‘Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar: Writings and Speeches’ in English.

15

Mahaparinirvan

He had diabetes from 1948 and was gravely ill in 1954. Three days after finishing his last manuscript, ‘The Buddha and Karl Marx’ (2 December 1956), he attained Mahaparinirvan on 6 December 1956 in his sleep in Delhi. A special aircraft brought his body to ‘Rajgruha’ in Mumbai, and on 7 December a Buddhist-style cremation was held at Dadar Chowpatty with lakhs attending. The place is now Chaityabhoomi. At the cremation, over a million followers took Buddhist diksha.

Every year more than 2 million people gather on 14 April, 6 December and 14 October at Chaityabhoomi (Mumbai), Deekshabhoomi (Nagpur) and Bhim Janmabhoomi (Mhow) to pay tribute.

16

Influence and Legacy

In independent India his socio-political ideas are respected across the political spectrum. Since 1927 followers have called him ‘Babasaheb’ (“respected father”), and the name ‘Bhim’ lives on in Jai Bhim, the Bhim flag, Bhim Janmabhoomi and more.

Columbian Ahead of Their Time

In 2004 first name on Columbia University’s list of 100 outstanding alumni; “the Maker of Modern India”.

The Greatest Indian · 2012

Most votes in a History TV18 and CNN-IBN poll (about 20 million votes cast).

Bharat Ratna · 1990

The country’s highest civilian honour, awarded posthumously.

Constitution Day · 26 November

The first formal Constitution Day was observed on 26 November 2015.

Students’ Day · 7 November

In Maharashtra’s schools, in memory of his school admission.

Worldwide

Jayanti is held in 65+ countries; his 125th jayanti in 2016 was marked in 102 countries and at the United Nations.

Memorials and Symbols

Jai Bhim — this greeting was coined by Babu L.N. Hardas. Blue stands for equality and the vastness of the sky; the blue flag with the Ashoka Chakra became the identity of the Ambedkarite movement. India Post issued stamps on him in 1966, 1973, 1991, 2001 and 2013; Google made a doodle on 14 April 2015. In 2023 a 125-ft statue was installed in Hyderabad and in 2024 another 125-ft statue in Vijayawada.
